# Chapter 1: World Art -- Design

Part of making Dope Sick feel like an AA indie game, in three chapters:
**1. world art** (this spec), 2. polish and presentation, 3. story and
characters. Each gets its own spec, plan and build.

## Intent

The people are now realistic (Microsoft Rocketbox), and the world around
them isn't: the street is Kenney's toy city kit stretched 5x, parked cars
and most furniture are flat-coloured boxes, store shelves are boxes. Make
the street and the places you spend the most time look like real, gritty
places, without changing how the game plays.

**Said by the partner:** all of it, step by step; world art first; street
plus key interiors; approach A (real props on the existing layouts); the
mock-up (`mockup_tmp/comparison.png`) is the right direction.

**Assumed:** the dark, grounded tone stays; everything shipped is free to
redistribute (CC0/MIT); it must run on the partner's laptop (Intel UHD
620).

## Scope

In: the street (City3D), the apartment, the Dive Bar, the five stores
(shared `_store_shell` builder).

Out (a later pass): jail, pawnshop, shelter, Tape Deck, kart centre,
backyard. Characters (done). Gameplay, layouts, UI, story.

## Hard constraints

- **Gameplay is untouched.** Collision boxes, sightline blockers, bounds,
  spawn markers, doors, interaction zones, guard placements and the baked
  navmesh stay where they are. Only visible meshes and materials change.
  The full smoke test (518 checks) must stay green.
- **Performance budget:** City3D and DiveBar3D on the Medium preset may get
  at most ~10% slower (GPU time per frame), measured by alternating A/B in
  one process as in the earlier performance work. Over budget means
  lower-detail pieces, fewer repeats, or detail only near the camera --
  not a slower game.
- **Licences:** Poly Haven (CC0), ambientCG (CC0), a CC0 car pack (licence
  checked on its download page before use). Credited in the asset folders.

## 1. The street

- **Facades** from Poly Haven's modular kits, assembled per building by a
  new builder helper fitted to each front's width, door and sign:
  `modular_urban_apartments_facade` for the tall buildings,
  `modular_factory_facade` for the low ones (Karts, Pawn, Tape Deck,
  Shelter). Upper floors: window walls, dado bands, cornices, crown. The
  Kenney building instances are removed.
- **Ground floors** carry most of what the top-down camera sees: shop
  windows (glass with a lit interior glow), metal frames, roller shutters
  (`rollershutter_window_*`, `rollershutter_door`) that close when the shop
  is closed, the existing neon signs, opening-hours plates, posters and
  doors kept where they are.
- **Street furniture:** fire escapes on the apartment blocks, gutters and
  drainpipes, wall air-con units, Poly Haven street lamps replacing the box
  lamps (light sources and their positions kept), electricity poles and
  cables, security cameras over shops, manhole covers, the hidden-alley
  set (chain-link fence, barrel stove, utility boxes), more trash and
  stains along the walls.
- **Cars:** parked cars become Poly Haven's `covered_car` and low-poly CC0
  sedans in muted colours; traffic (StreetLife.gd) uses the same pack
  instead of Kenney's cars.
- **Ground:** ambientCG PBR for the sidewalk (cracked concrete slabs) and
  road (worn asphalt with lane markings), and a curb along the edge.

## 2. The interiors

- **Apartment:** an old bed frame with a stained mattress and crumpled
  blankets, Poly Haven's worn sofa, a real old TV and radio, a wooden
  chair, a metal bin, a bare bulb on a pull-chain socket, a wall clock.
  The belongings (TV, radio, guitar, coat, ring) get real models, and
  keep their interaction zones and taken/pawned/gone logic.
- **Dive Bar:** counter and back bar in wood PBR, a back-bar shelf of real
  bottles under the mirror, round and metal bar stools, worn wooden
  tables, a hanging industrial lamp over the pool table; booths, pool
  table and jukebox keep their shapes with vinyl, felt and wood
  materials.
- **Stores:** `steel_frame_shelves` units replace the box shelves (the
  sight-blocking colliders stay), real fluorescent fittings replace the
  glowing strip boxes, cash registers, security cameras, a wet-floor sign,
  a metal rack or cart at the back; fridges and coolers keep their shapes
  with glass, brushed metal and an inner glow; ambientCG floors and walls
  (worn linoleum, painted cinder block). Stealable products unchanged.

## 3. How it's built

- **Assets:** `dev-tools/fetch_polyhaven_models.py` gains the new models
  (1k textures); a new `dev-tools/fetch_ambientcg.py` fetches the PBR
  materials into `assets/pbr/`; the car pack goes in `assets/cars/`.
- **Builder:** the rooms are generated by `dev-tools/build_rooms_3d.gd`;
  the changes go there and the scenes are regenerated (committing only the
  scenes each step changes). A `_facade()` helper builds fronts from kit
  pieces, measuring each piece's bounds so it sits flush on the building
  line, like the mock-up.
- **Order, each its own commit with before/after screenshots for the
  partner:** 1 street, 2 apartment, 3 Dive Bar, 4 stores.

## 4. Testing

- Full smoke test green after each step.
- New checks (`_world_art_checks`, runnable alone): no Kenney city
  buildings or box cars remain on the street; every front has a facade; the
  apartment, bar and store fixtures use the new models; gameplay nodes
  (doors, zones, colliders, spawn markers) still exist at their old
  positions.
- Performance A/B for City3D and DiveBar3D on Medium after steps 1 and 3,
  recorded in the commit message.
- Pilot screenshots of each changed place, before and after, opened on the
  partner's screen.

## Risks

- Facade kit pieces are big; twelve fronts could cost too much GPU. The
  A/B budget catches it; the fix is fewer, simpler pieces.
- Regenerating scenes rewrites `.tscn` unique ids; only the scenes a step
  changes are committed.
- Sizes and pivots of new models vary; each is placed by measured bounds,
  and checked in screenshots.
